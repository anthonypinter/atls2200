Choose elements for meaning

HTML has two dozen elements for text, and the trick is to choose them for what the text means, not how it looks. The look is CSS's job, and you can always change it. The meaning is what screen readers, search engines and browser reading modes rely on.

Start with headings. They form an outline of the page: one <h1> for the page's main title, <h2> for each section, <h3> for parts of a section. Screen reader users jump between headings to scan a page, the way you skim bold titles, so don't skip levels just because <h4> looks the right size. Pick the right level, then resize it with CSS.

<h1>Royal Arch Trail</h1>
<h2>Before you go</h2>
<p><strong>Bring water.</strong> There's none on the trail.</p>
<h2>The route</h2>

Inside paragraphs, pairs of elements look alike but mean different things:

  • <strong> means important, and <b> just draws attention without extra importance.
  • <em> means stressed, the word you'd say louder, and <i> sets text apart, like a scientific name or a book title in another language.

For quotations, use <blockquote> for a long, standalone quote and <q> for a short one inside a sentence. <cite> marks the title of a work, and <code> marks a piece of code.

Tip

If you're reaching for an element because of its default look, stop and ask what the text is. The answer tells you the element; CSS handles the rest. This is the core idea of semantic HTML.

    Key ideas

    • One <h1> per page, then <h2> for sections and <h3> inside those. Don't skip levels.
    • <strong> means important and <em> means stressed. <b> and <i> only change the look.
    • Pick elements for meaning, then change the look with CSS. A heading can be small; strong text doesn't have to be bold.
    • Use <p> for each paragraph instead of <br><br> between them.

    Common mistakes

    • Picking <h4> because you want smaller text.
    • Making a fake heading with bold text in a <p>.
    • Using <blockquote> just to indent something.
    • Wrapping whole paragraphs in <strong>. When everything is important, nothing is.
