Transitions & Animations

A transition smooths the change between two states, like on hover. An animation runs a sequence of keyframes by itself. Small, quick motion helps people notice changes; too much distracts.

Your device is set to reduce motion. This page plays its demos anyway so you can learn from them, but the rest of this site turns motion off for you. Your own sites should do the same with a prefers-reduced-motion media query.

Key ideas

  • Put transition on the element's normal state, not the :hover state, so it animates both ways.
  • Most interface motion should take 150–400ms. Longer feels sluggish.
  • transform and opacity animate smoothly. Animating width, top or left makes the browser redo layout every frame.
  • Wrap big motion in @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) so people who get motion sick are spared.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to transition display: none. It can't fade; use opacity instead.
  • Writing transition: all and animating things you didn't mean to.
  • Forgetting the unit: transition-duration: 300 does nothing. Write 300ms or 0.3s.
  • Infinite animations on content people are trying to read.
