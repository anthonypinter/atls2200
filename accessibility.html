Accessibility Basics

An accessible site works for people using a keyboard, a screen reader, zoom, or a phone in bright sun. This sample page has seven common problems. Click into it and press Tab to move through it, then compare it with the fixed version.

    Your turn

    Five checks for your own project

    • Unplug your mouseUse Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter and Space. Can you reach and use everything? Can you always see where you are?
    • Zoom to 200%Press Ctrl or Cmd and + a few times. Nothing should overlap or get cut off.
    • Check contrastBody text needs at least 4.5 : 1 against its background. The Colors & Units page can check it.
    • Read the headings aloneDo h1, h2 and h3 make a sensible outline of the page, in order?
    • Turn images offDoes the alt text tell you what you'd miss? See the Images & Alt Text page.

    Key ideas

    • Use the right HTML element first. Real buttons, links, labels and headings come with accessibility built in.
    • Everything you can do with a mouse should work with a keyboard.
    • Every control needs a name a screen reader can say: label text, button text or alt text.
    • Don't use color alone to show meaning, like red for errors. Add text or an icon.

    Common mistakes

    • Clickable divs and spans. They can't be reached with Tab.
    • outline: none with no replacement focus style.
    • Placeholder text used as the only label.
    • Pale gray text because it "looks clean."
