Overflow

Overflow happens when content is bigger than its box. CSS doesn't hide it by default; it lets it spill out so you notice. Here's how to control it, and how to track down the element that makes a whole page scroll sideways.

You'll be able to

  • Control content that doesn't fit with overflow: visible, auto, hidden and clip.
  • Shorten one-line text with an ellipsis, and let long words and links wrap.
  • Track down the element that makes a whole page scroll sideways.

When content doesn't fit

Overflow is what happens when content is bigger than its box: a long paragraph in a short card, or a giant image in a narrow column. CSS's default is to let it spill out, visibly, so you notice the problem instead of silently losing content.

The overflow property decides what happens instead:

  • visible, the default, lets it spill out.
  • auto adds a scrollbar only when it's needed. It's usually the right choice.
  • hidden and clip cut the extra content off. Be sure nothing important gets lost.
  • scroll always shows scrollbars, even with nothing to scroll, so it's rarely what you want.

Two text problems come up constantly. A title that must stay on one line can end in an ellipsis instead of spilling:

.title {
  white-space: nowrap;
  overflow: hidden;
  text-overflow: ellipsis;
}

And a long URL or word has no spaces to break at, so it pushes its box wider. overflow-wrap: anywhere lets it break where it needs to.

The most common overflow bug is a whole page that scrolls sideways on a phone. Somewhere, one element is wider than the screen: a fixed width: 400px, a big image, a long link. The fix is to find that element and make it flexible, not to hide the scrollbar.

Watch out

overflow: hidden on the body hides the sideways scroll, but the content is still too wide and now some of it is unreachable. Find the cause with DevTools instead.

Tame the overflow

Three boxes, three kinds of overflow. Pick the right fix for each.

Key ideas

  • overflow: auto adds a scrollbar only when needed. It's usually the best choice.
  • hidden and clip cut content off. Make sure nothing important is lost.
  • Prefer min-height to height for boxes with text, so they can grow.
  • Long URLs and words don't wrap by themselves. overflow-wrap: anywhere lets them break.

Common mistakes

  • Fixed pixel widths, like width: 380px, wider than a phone screen.
  • Putting overflow: hidden on body to hide the sideways scroll instead of fixing the cause.
  • Fixed height on a text box that later gets more text, translated text, or larger font settings.
  • Using overflow: scroll, which shows scrollbars even when nothing overflows.
