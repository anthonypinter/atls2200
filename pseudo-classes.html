Styling states and positions

A pseudo-class selects an element when it's in a certain state, or in a certain position. It's written with one colon after a normal selector:

.btn:hover,
.btn:focus-visible {
  background: #1a2fa8;
}
li:nth-child(even) {
  background: #f2f4fa;
}

State pseudo-classes respond to what the visitor is doing. :hover applies while the mouse is over an element, :active while it's being pressed, and :focus when it's ready for keyboard input. Forms add more, like :checked and :invalid.

Notice that the first rule styles :hover and :focus-visible together. Many people use a keyboard instead of a mouse, pressing Tab to move between links and buttons. :focus-visible shows them where they are, the way hover feedback shows a mouse user. Style the two together and nobody misses out. And since phones have no hover at all, never hide anything important behind :hover alone.

Position pseudo-classes pick elements by where they sit among their siblings. :first-child and :last-child do what they say. :nth-child() takes a pattern: odd, even, a number, or a formula like 3n for every third item.

Links have four states, and their order in your stylesheet matters because later rules win: :link, :visited, :hover, :active. A handy way to remember it is "LoVe HAte".

Watch out

Never remove the focus outline with outline: none unless you add a clear :focus-visible style in its place. Without it, keyboard users can't see where they are on the page.

    Key ideas

    • Pseudo-classes use one colon (:hover). Pseudo-elements like ::before use two.
    • Style :hover and :focus-visible together so keyboard users get the same feedback as mouse users.
    • Phones have no hover. Never hide something important behind :hover alone.
    • :nth-child(odd) and (even) are great for striped tables and lists.
    • For security, browsers only let :visited change colors, nothing else.

    Common mistakes

    • Removing focus outlines with outline: none and adding nothing back, so keyboard users get lost.
    • A space before the colon: a :hover means "any hovered element inside an a."
    • Expecting p:first-child to pick the first p. It picks a p only if it's the very first child of its parent.
    • Showing :invalid styles before the user has typed anything.
