Pseudo-elements

Pseudo-elements, written with two colons, style a part of an element, or add decorative content before or after it, without changing your HTML. Switch each one on and off to see what it adds.

Read

Style a part, or add a decoration

A pseudo-element lets you style a part of an element, or add decoration to it, without touching your HTML. It's written with two colons, which is how you tell it apart from a pseudo-class like :hover.

The two you'll use most are ::before and ::after. They insert a box at the start or end of an element's content:

.quote::before {
  content: "“";
  font-size: 3rem;
  color: #b4410c;
}

Every ::before and ::after needs a content property, or it doesn't appear at all. Often the content is empty, content: "", and the pseudo-element is just a box you style into an underline, an arrow or a colored bar.

Others select a part of what's already there:

  • ::first-letter styles the first letter, for a drop cap.
  • ::first-line styles the first line, however long it ends up being.
  • ::marker styles a list item's bullet or number.
  • ::selection styles text the visitor highlights.
  • ::placeholder styles the hint text in an input.

Pseudo-elements are for decoration. Text in content can't be selected or copied, and screen readers don't treat it consistently, so anything visitors need to read belongs in the HTML.

Note

::before and ::after go inside the element, as its first and last child. That's why they don't work on <img> or <input>: those elements have no inside to put them in.

Key ideas

  • ::before and ::after need a content property, even if it's empty (content: ""), or they don't appear.
  • They're inserted inside the element, as its first and last child.
  • Use them for decoration: quote marks, icons, underlines, arrows. Don't put important information in them.
  • Two colons mark a pseudo-element (::before). One colon is a pseudo-class (:hover).

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting content, then wondering why ::after does nothing.
  • Trying ::before on an <img> or <input>. Elements with no content inside can't have them.
  • Putting real text in content. It can't be selected and some screen readers skip it.
  • Expecting ::first-letter to work on an inline element like a span.
