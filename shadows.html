Shadows & Filters

Shadows make flat boxes look lifted off the page, and filters change how an element is drawn, like a photo editor. A little goes a long way: soft, subtle shadows look the most natural.

box-shadow vs filter: drop-shadow()

box-shadow always follows the element's rectangle. The drop-shadow filter follows the actual shape, including transparent parts of an image.

Key ideas

  • Use a semi-transparent shadow color like rgb(0 0 0 / 15%). Solid black looks harsh.
  • Shadows coming slightly down (positive y) look natural, like light from above.
  • Stack shadows with commas: a tight dark one plus a wide soft one looks realistic.
  • Shadows don't take up space or move other elements. They're painted on top.

Common mistakes

  • Heavy black shadows on everything, so nothing stands out.
  • Relying on a shadow alone to separate a card from a similar background. Add a border too.
  • Text shadows on body text. Save them for big text on images.
  • Animating box-shadow on many elements, which can make scrolling choppy.
