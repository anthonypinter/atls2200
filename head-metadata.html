The Head & Metadata

Nothing in the <head> shows on the page, but it decides how your site looks everywhere else: the browser tab, search results, and the preview card when someone shares a link.

Favicons
Make the little tab icon
A favicon is a small square image. One SVG covers most browsers. Add a PNG for phones that save your site to the home screen.

  

    
1. Make a square image
Use a simple shape or one or two letters. Fine detail disappears at 16 × 16 pixels. Save it as favicon.svg, or as a 32 × 32 favicon.png.

    
2. Link it in the head
<link rel="icon" href="favicon.svg"
      type="image/svg+xml">

    
3. Add a home-screen icon
<link rel="apple-touch-icon"
      href="icon-180.png">
A 180 × 180 PNG, used when someone adds your site to their phone's home screen.

  


  

    

      
Key ideas

      
    
        
  • Give every page its own <title>, most specific part first: "Royal Arch Trail | Boulder Trail Club". It's also what bookmarks and screen readers announce.
    • 
        
  • The meta description is a one-sentence pitch of about 150 characters. Search engines may show it under your title.
    • 
        
  • Open Graph (og:) tags control the preview card in chats and social posts. Without them, apps guess, or show a bare link.
    • 
        
  • og:image needs a full https:// URL, not a relative path, because the image is fetched by another company's server.
    • 
        
  • A good share image is 1200 × 630 pixels.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Common mistakes

      
    
        
  • Copying one page to start another and leaving the same title on every page.
    • 
        
  • Putting <meta> or <link> tags in the body. They only work in the head.
    • 
        
  • Expecting <meta name="keywords"> to help. Search engines have ignored it for years.
    • 
        
  • Changing the favicon and not seeing it. Browsers cache favicons hard: try a private window.
