The Head & Metadata

Nothing in the <head> shows on the page, but it decides how your site looks everywhere else: the browser tab, search results, and the preview card when someone shares a link.

You'll be able to

  • Write a page title and meta description that work in tabs and search results.
  • Add a favicon to a site.
  • Use Open Graph tags to control the preview card when a link is shared.
Read

What the head controls

Nothing in the head appears on your page, but it decides how your site shows up everywhere else: in the browser tab, in bookmarks, in search results, and in the little preview card when someone pastes your link into a chat.

Four things do most of the work:

  • <title> is the text in the tab and the clickable headline in search results. Make it specific, most important part first: "Royal Arch Trail | Boulder Trail Club".
  • The meta description is a one-sentence summary, about 150 characters, that search engines may show under the title.
  • A favicon is the small icon in the tab, linked with <link rel="icon">.
  • Open Graph tags, written og:, control the preview card in messages and social posts: its title, description and image.
<title>Royal Arch Trail | Boulder Trail Club</title>
<meta name="description" content="A steep 3.4-mile hike to a sandstone arch above Boulder.">
<link rel="icon" href="favicon.svg">
<meta property="og:image" content="https://trails.example.com/img/royal-arch.jpg">

Notice that the og:image is a full address starting with https://. The preview card is built by another company's server, which has no idea where your page lives, so a relative path like img/royal-arch.jpg won't work there.

Watch out

When you copy one page to start another, the head comes along too. It's easy to end up with ten pages all titled "Home". Change the title and description on every new page.

Favicons
Make the little tab icon
A favicon is a small square image. One SVG covers most browsers. Add a PNG for phones that save your site to the home screen.

  

    
1. Make a square image
Use a simple shape or one or two letters. Fine detail disappears at 16 × 16 pixels. Save it as favicon.svg, or as a 32 × 32 favicon.png.

    
2. Link it in the head
<link rel="icon" href="favicon.svg"
      type="image/svg+xml">

    
3. Add a home-screen icon
<link rel="apple-touch-icon"
      href="icon-180.png">
A 180 × 180 PNG, used when someone adds your site to their phone's home screen.

Key ideas

      
    
        
  • Give every page its own <title>, most specific part first: "Royal Arch Trail | Boulder Trail Club". It's also what bookmarks and screen readers announce.
    • 
        
  • The meta description is a one-sentence pitch of about 150 characters. Search engines may show it under your title.
    • 
        
  • Open Graph (og:) tags control the preview card in chats and social posts. Without them, apps guess, or show a bare link.
    • 
        
  • og:image needs a full https:// URL, not a relative path, because the image is fetched by another company's server.
    • 
        
  • A good share image is 1200 × 630 pixels.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Common mistakes

      
    
        
  • Copying one page to start another and leaving the same title on every page.
    • 
        
  • Putting <meta> or <link> tags in the body. They only work in the head.
    • 
        
  • Expecting <meta name="keywords"> to help. Search engines have ignored it for years.
    • 
        
  • Changing the favicon and not seeing it. Browsers cache favicons hard: try a private window.
